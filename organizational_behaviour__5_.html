Unit I

Fundamentals of Organizational Behaviour

📖 Definition, Nature & Goals of OB
Organizational Behaviour (OB) is the systematic study and application of knowledge about how individuals and groups act within the organizations where they work. — Stephen P. Robbins

OB integrates knowledge from psychology, sociology, anthropology, political science, and economics to understand behaviour in workplace settings.

NatureAn interdisciplinary applied science; empirical and goal-oriented.
ScopeIndividual, group, and organizational-level behaviour.
Goal 1Describe human behaviour at work accurately.
Goal 2Understand why behaviour occurs.
Goal 3Predict future behaviour patterns.
Goal 4Control and manage behaviour to improve performance.
Interdisciplinary Applied Science Goal-Oriented Humanistic Empirical
🧱 Fundamental Concepts of OB

OB rests on a foundation of six key concepts that shape our understanding of workplace behaviour.

1
Individual Differences

Every person is unique. OB respects this through a personalized approach to management rather than one-size-fits-all rules.

2
A Whole Person

Employees bring their whole self to work — skills, personal life, emotions, family background — all of which affect performance.

3
Motivated Behaviour

People are motivated by needs. OB helps managers understand these needs to channel behaviour toward organizational goals.

4
Value of the Person (Human Dignity)

People deserve to be treated with respect and dignity — not merely as tools for achieving organizational ends.

5
Organizations as Social Systems

Organizations function as social systems governed by both formal rules and informal norms. Relationships are dynamic and interdependent.

6
Mutuality of Interest

Organizations need people; people need organizations. Healthy OB builds symbiotic relationships between both parties.

🌍 Emerging Aspects: Cultural Diversity & Perception

Cultural Diversity in OB refers to the variety of human societies, cultures, ethnic groups, and identities present in an organization.

Surface-Level Diversity Age, gender, race/ethnicity, physical ability — observable characteristics.
Deep-Level Diversity Personality, values, beliefs, attitudes — less visible but more impactful.
Benefits of Diversity Broader perspectives, creativity, better problem-solving, wider talent pool.
Managing Diversity Inclusive leadership, cultural sensitivity training, equitable policies.

Managing the Perception Process involves being aware of perceptual biases (stereotyping, halo effect, selective perception) and actively working to make fair, objective judgments about people and situations in the workplace.

Perception, Attitude, Values & Motivation

👁️ Perception: Concept, Nature & Process
Perception is the process by which individuals organize and interpret their sensory impressions in order to give meaning to their environment.

Perception is important because behaviour is based on perception of reality, not on reality itself. Two people can see the same thing and interpret it differently.

💡 Attitude, Values & Job Satisfaction
An attitude is a learned predisposition to respond in a consistently favorable or unfavorable manner toward a given object, person, or event.

Components of Attitude (ABC Model):

A — AffectiveEmotional/feeling component ("I feel anxious about this policy")
B — BehaviouralIntended actions ("I will avoid that coworker")
C — CognitiveBelief/knowledge component ("I believe the policy is unfair")

Effects of Employee Attitudes: Positive attitudes → higher productivity, better teamwork, lower absenteeism. Negative attitudes → poor performance, high turnover, conflicts.

Values are enduring beliefs about what is important. Personal values reflect individual priorities (family, success, freedom); organizational values define culture and guide decisions (integrity, innovation, customer focus).

Job Satisfaction is a pleasurable or positive emotional state resulting from one's appraisal of job and job experiences. Key determinants: pay, work itself, promotion, supervision, co-workers, working conditions.

🔥 Nature & Importance of Motivation
Motivation is an internal force that drives an individual to behave in a certain way to satisfy an unsatisfied need. It energizes, directs, and sustains behaviour toward a goal.
NatureInternal process; complex and individual-specific; goal-directed; persistent; socially influenced.
ImportanceImproves performance, reduces absenteeism, builds loyalty, fosters creativity, and aligns individual–org goals.
Achievement MotiveMcClelland's nAch: a strong desire to accomplish challenging tasks and excel. High-achievers prefer moderate risk and immediate feedback.

⚖️ McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y

Douglas McGregor (1960) proposed two contrasting sets of assumptions managers hold about their employees:

Theory X — Pessimistic View Employees are inherently lazy, dislike work, avoid responsibility, need close supervision, and are motivated primarily by money and fear of punishment. Management style: Authoritarian.
Theory Y — Optimistic View Employees are self-motivated, seek responsibility, are creative, exercise self-direction, and find work natural. Management style: Participative, decentralized decision-making.
Managerial Implication: Theory Y assumptions lead to higher morale, commitment, and productivity. Modern OB strongly advocates a Theory Y orientation while recognizing situational flexibility.
Unit III

Personality

🧩 Definition & Determinants of Personality
Personality is the sum total of ways in which an individual reacts to and interacts with others. It is the dynamic organization of psychophysical systems that determines characteristic behaviour, thoughts, and feelings. — Gordon Allport

Determinants of Personality:

HeredityGenes, biological makeup, chromosomal structure. Sets the outer limits of personality development.
EnvironmentCulture, family, social groups, norms, experiences — shapes personality within hereditary limits.
Family BackgroundParenting styles, siblings, socioeconomic status, early socialization experiences.
Situational FactorsDifferent situations bring out different traits; personality is not static across all contexts.
🔬 Trait and Type Theories

Trait Theories identify specific characteristics (traits) that describe personality. Traits are relatively stable, consistent ways of behaving.

Allport's Trait Theory classifies traits as: Cardinal (dominant, life-defining), Central (general characteristics, ~5–10 per person), and Secondary (situational, less consistent).

Type Theories classify people into discrete categories or "types." Sheldon's somatotype theory (endomorph/mesomorph/ectomorph) linked body type to personality — largely rejected today. More influential: introvert vs. extravert (Jung).

🧭 MBTI, Locus of Control & Type A/B

The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) measures personality along four dichotomies:

E vs IExtraversion (outer world) vs. Introversion (inner world)
S vs NSensing (concrete facts) vs. Intuition (patterns & possibilities)
T vs FThinking (logic) vs. Feeling (values & people)
J vs PJudging (structured) vs. Perceiving (flexible)

This yields 16 personality types (e.g., INTJ, ENFP). Used widely in career counselling and team composition.

Locus of Control Internal LOC: Belief that outcomes are controlled by oneself → higher motivation, better performance.

External LOC: Belief in fate, luck, or others' control → lower initiative, more stress.
Type A vs Type B Type A: Competitive, time-pressured, impatient, aggressive — high CHD risk, strong performance but prone to burnout.

Type B: Relaxed, patient, easygoing — better long-term health, creative thinkers.
Unit IV

Work Stress

⚡ Meaning, Definition & Symptoms of Stress
Stress is a dynamic condition in which an individual is confronted with an opportunity, constraint, or demand related to what they desire and for which the outcome is perceived to be both uncertain and important. — Robbins & Judge

Hans Selye (1936) described stress as the "non-specific response of the body to any demand made upon it," coining the GAS model (General Adaptation Syndrome): Alarm → Resistance → Exhaustion.

Symptoms of Stress:

PhysiologicalHeadaches, high BP, heart disease, ulcers, fatigue, muscle tension.
PsychologicalAnxiety, depression, irritability, job dissatisfaction, boredom.
BehaviouralReduced productivity, absenteeism, turnover, substance abuse, sleep disorders.
🌪️ Sources of Stress & Stressors
Individual Level
  • Role conflict & ambiguity
  • Work overload/underload
  • Lack of autonomy
  • Career development concerns
  • Personal life events
Group Level
  • Poor group dynamics
  • Interpersonal conflicts
  • Lack of social support
  • Status incongruence
  • Group cohesiveness issues
Organizational Level
  • Organizational culture
  • Leadership style
  • Poor communication
  • Technology change
  • Downsizing/restructuring
Extra-Organizational
  • Family/marital problems
  • Economic difficulties
  • Rapid social change
  • Community issues
  • Housing, commuting
Burnout is a state of chronic stress that leads to physical and emotional exhaustion, cynicism and detachment, and feelings of ineffectiveness. It is the ultimate negative outcome of unmanaged occupational stress.
🛡️ Stress Management Strategies
Individual Strategies Time management · Exercise & physical fitness · Relaxation techniques (yoga, meditation) · Social support networks · Cognitive restructuring (change thinking patterns) · Biofeedback · Proper nutrition and sleep · Hobbies and leisure
Organizational Strategies Improved communication · Role clarity · Reasonable workloads · Employee wellness programs · Flexible work arrangements · Supportive supervision · Team building · EAP (Employee Assistance Programs)
💬 Employee Counselling
Employee counselling is a discussion of an emotional problem with an employee with the general goal of decreasing it. It involves a trained counsellor (or manager) helping the employee understand their situation and develop coping strategies.
Directive CounsellingCounsellor-led; advises, directs, and reassures. Best for specific, immediate problems.
Non-Directive CounsellingEmployee-led; counsellor listens, reflects, and facilitates self-discovery. Deeper personal issues.
Cooperative CounsellingBalanced approach combining elements of both directive and non-directive methods.
FunctionsAdvice, reassurance, communication, release of emotional tension, reorientation.

Group Behaviour & Leadership

👥 Nature & Types of Groups
A group is defined as two or more individuals, interacting and interdependent, who come together to achieve particular objectives.
Formal Groups Defined by organizational structure with designated roles. Subtypes: Command Group (boss + direct reports), Task Group (cross-functional, project-based).
Informal Groups Naturally formed for social needs, not organizational mandate. Subtypes: Interest Group (shared goal), Friendship Group (social affinity).

👑 Leadership: Nature, Styles & Traits
Leadership is the process of influencing people and providing an environment for them to achieve team or organizational objectives. It involves vision, inspiration, and guiding others toward goals.

Classical Leadership Styles:

AutocraticLeader makes all decisions alone; fast but stifles creativity. Best in crisis situations.
DemocraticTeam participates in decisions; higher morale and creativity but slower.
Laissez-faireHands-off; full autonomy to team. Works with highly skilled, self-motivated individuals.
TransformationalInspires change through vision; motivates beyond self-interest. Builds long-term commitment.
TransactionalExchange-based; rewards and punishments tied to performance targets.
Servant LeadershipLeader prioritizes followers' needs; builds trust and community.

Traits of Effective Leaders:

Intelligence Self-Confidence Determination Integrity Sociability Emotional Intelligence Visionary Decisiveness Empathy Communication Adaptability Accountability
Unit VI

Conflict in Organizations

⚔️ Nature & Process of Conflict
Conflict is a process that begins when one party perceives that another party has negatively affected, or is about to negatively affect, something the first party cares about. — Robbins

Views of Conflict:

Traditional ViewConflict is bad, harmful, disruptive — must be avoided and eliminated.
Human Relations ViewConflict is natural and inevitable in any group; can be accepted.
Interactionist ViewSome conflict is necessary for group performance — promotes creativity and self-evaluation.

Process of Conflict (Pondy's Model):

Latent ConflictConditions exist
Perceived ConflictAwareness
Felt ConflictEmotional involvement
Manifest ConflictOpen behaviour
Conflict AftermathResolution or escalation
🏔️ Levels of Conflict & Sources
Intrapersonal Conflict Within a single individual. Types:
• Approach–Approach: Choice between two positives
• Avoidance–Avoidance: Two equally undesirable options
• Approach–Avoidance: One goal with both positive and negative aspects
• Role conflict: multiple incompatible role demands
Interpersonal Conflict Between two or more individuals. Caused by personality differences, communication breakdowns, status differences, resource competition, goal incompatibility, and differing values.

Sources of Conflict:

Resource Scarcity Goal Incompatibility Communication Failure Value Differences Status Ambiguity Structural Factors Personal Grudges Role Ambiguity
Effects of Conflict: Positive — stimulates creativity, identifies problems, promotes change, increases group cohesion (against external threat). Negative — stress, burnout, reduced communication, hostility, organisational inefficiency.
🕊️ Conflict Resolution

Thomas-Kilmann model identifies five conflict-handling styles based on assertiveness (concern for self) and cooperativeness (concern for others):

StyleAssertivenessCooperativenessWhen to Use
CompetingHighLowEmergency; clear right/wrong; unpopular but necessary decisions
CollaboratingHighHighLong-term relationships; complex issues requiring creative solutions
CompromisingMediumMediumTemporary solutions; equal power; time-pressured situations
AvoidingLowLowTrivial issues; when others can resolve it better
AccommodatingLowHighWhen you're wrong; issue more important to other party; goodwill building

Other Resolution Mechanisms: Mediation (neutral third party facilitates), Arbitration (binding third-party decision), Negotiation, Superordinate goals (shared goals transcend conflict), Structural changes (redesigning roles/departments).

📋 Grievances: Meaning, Types & Process
A grievance is any real or perceived feeling of dissatisfaction or injustice in the workplace that an employee feels about their employment relationship, and desires management to resolve.
Factual GrievanceGenuine reason exists; management has failed to meet obligations (e.g., withheld promised promotion).
Imaginary GrievanceBased on misunderstanding or wrong perception; still needs to be addressed respectfully.
Disguised GrievanceReal cause is unknown even to the employee; may stem from personal or psychological problems.

Common Causes: Wage/salary issues, working conditions, promotions, disciplinary actions, leave policies, supervisory behavior, transfer issues.

Grievance Handling Process (Open-Door / Step-Ladder):
